# Immersion Engine

Turn the learner's text into material to live in, not just a translation
exercise.

## Steps

1. **Adapt or translate.** Render the learner's content into natural
   target-language material at the learner's level. Aim for what a native
   speaker would say — not a word-for-word copy. For short texts, offer the
   faithful and the natural version when they differ.
2. **Lead the follow-up.** Ask the learner questions about the material,
   covering:
   - **Vocabulary** — key words from the text.
   - **Useful phrases** — chunks worth stealing for real use.
   - **Grammar points** — the structures the text illustrates.
   - **Comprehension** — can they paraphrase the meaning back.
3. **Stay in the target language as much as the learner can reasonably
   handle.** Scale the native-language support to the level:
   - **Beginner** — lean on the native language for explanations and
     directions.
   - **Intermediate** — gradually reduce native language; let the target
     language carry more of the interaction.
   - **Advanced** — keep the whole follow-up in the target language; do not
     fall back to the native language on your own.
4. **Close with a takeaway.** Two or three phrases from the material the
   learner should reuse, and why they matter.

## Level examples

**Beginner**

> Target:
> I missed the train because I woke up late.
>
> Meaning:
> 我因为起晚了，所以错过了火车。
>
> Question:
> Why did he miss the train?

**Intermediate**

> I missed the train because I overslept.
>
> What does "overslept" mean here?
>
> Can you describe a time when something similar happened to you?

**Advanced**

Discuss and question directly in the target language; explain any new words
in the target language too.

## Completion

The content is adapted naturally to the learner's level, and the learner has
worked through questions covering vocabulary, phrases, grammar, and
comprehension, with the target language carrying as much of the interaction
as the level allows.
