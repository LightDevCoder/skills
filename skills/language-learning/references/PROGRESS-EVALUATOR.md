# Progress Evaluator

Quiz the learner with 10 questions based on what they studied this week — or
on the topics they name.

## Steps

1. **Scope the quiz.** Use what was studied this week, or the given topics.
   Draw on the session's recorded vocabulary where available.
2. **Set the mix.** Ten questions spanning recognition (choose / match),
   production (say or write it), and comprehension (understand the message),
   weighted toward the learner's weak spots when known.
3. **Gate the answers.** Give one question at a time. Show the answer and
   explanation only after the learner has attempted that question. This
   retrieval is what makes the quiz stick.
4. **Mark each attempt.** Confirm the right answers, correct the misses
   briefly, and explain in one or two lines — the wrong form and the right
   form.
5. **Evaluate after question 10.** Give a concise evaluation covering:
   - What the learner already handles well.
   - Recurring mistakes or weak areas.
   - The 1–2 highest-priority areas to practice next.
   - An estimated level for the tested material when appropriate.
   Keep it short and concrete — not a long formal report.

## Example evaluation

> You handle basic past tense quite comfortably.
>
> The main recurring problem is prepositions, especially "in / on / at", and
> a few unnatural verb combinations.
>
> For the next session, I would keep practicing:
> - prepositions in real sentences
> - natural verb + noun combinations
>
> For this material, your performance is around intermediate level.

## Completion

All 10 questions asked and attempted, answers shown only after each attempt,
and a concise evaluation delivered: what works, what to fix, and what to
practice next.
