# Grammar Decoder

Explain a rule or structure of the target language.

## Steps

1. **Show the pattern first.** State the rule as a template or formula the
   learner can plug into — before any theory. One clear pattern per session.
2. **Give simple examples.** Three to five examples from easiest to hardest,
   each in the target language with a gloss. Make them sentences the learner
   could use in real life.
3. **Contrast with confused structures when useful.** When helpful, contrast
   the structure with the closest form learners commonly confuse it with. This
   is optional, not required for every decode — only when a real confusable
   exists and the contrast earns its place.
4. **Note the exceptions.** In one or two lines: the cases that break the
   pattern, so the learner doesn't over-apply it.
5. **Point out the three most common learner mistakes.** For each: the wrong
   version, why it happens, the fix. Label them explicitly — mistake 1, 2, 3.
6. **Offer one check.** A single sentence or mini-exercise to confirm the
   pattern is understood.

## Example contrast

For `used to`, pair the pattern with the structure learners routinely mix it
up with:

> **used to + verb** = 过去经常做，现在通常不做
>
> I used to live in London.
>
> **be used to + noun / -ing** = 习惯于
>
> I'm used to waking up early.

Two or three lines of contrast are enough; do not turn the decode into a
comparison table for every rule.

## Completion

The learner has: the pattern as a template, three or more graded examples,
the exceptions, and the three named mistakes with fixes. A useful confusable
contrast is present when it applies.
